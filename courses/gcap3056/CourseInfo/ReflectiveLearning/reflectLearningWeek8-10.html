The weekly routine (same every week)

  1. Do the task for the week and post your reply in the Moodle forum thread for that week.
  2. Draft your entry in your journal Google Doc — one tab per entry (Tab 3 = Week 8, Tab 4 = Week 9, Tab 5 = Week 10).
  3. Paste your doc link at gcap3056.simonsays.hk/reflection/ (name + group + link). Status should read ok; if it says error, set the doc to “Anyone with the link can view”.
  4. When your entry is final, copy it into the Moodle forum thread to submit.

You may use AI to draft — but you review, judge and revise every entry before submitting (human oversight). How you divide the work between you, AI, peers and the teacher is itself one of the things we want you to reflect on.

Week 8 — Entry 3 (required): Your learning setup and your partnership map

Moodle task: reply in the Week 8 journal thread. Answer all three parts (about 150 words in total, more if useful):

  1. Website setup feedback. Look at the course tools you actually used this week — gcap3056.simonsays.hk, the reflection status page, your group’s tracker page, Moodle, Google Docs. What is working? What is confusing or slow? Name one concrete change that would help you get the job done.
  2. Partnership map. Pick one task you did this week. Write three short columns: I did… / AI did… / Peers and teacher did…. Then say where you added something AI could not (judgment, context, a decision, a fix).
  3. Wow / Aha. One moment this week that surprised or impressed you (Wow), or one moment you saw how to apply a course idea to your project (Aha).

Week 9 — Entry 4 (optional, encouraged): From task to lasting change

Moodle task: reply in the Week 9 journal thread. Pick one:

  • (a) Before → after. How you approached one type of task (finding government information, drafting a request or letter, working in your group) at the start of the course vs. now. What changed in your behaviour or attitude, not just in the output?
  • (b) A failure worth keeping. Something that went wrong (an AI answer you nearly submitted, a request that stalled, a misunderstanding in your group). What did you learn, and what will you do differently?
  • (c) Your working method. The workflow you now rely on (tools, prompts, who reviews what, when you ask a peer or the teacher). Why do you trust it?

Week 10 — Entry 5 (optional, encouraged): Curate and commit

Moodle task: reply in the Week 10 journal thread.

  1. Curate. Choose your strongest material from your Moodle replies and earlier tabs. You may rewrite, merge or replace earlier entries — the final set should read as your learning story, not a log of tasks.
  2. Commit. Finish with one paragraph: what habit or mindset are you taking out of this course that will still be there next year? (This is the “lasting change” we grade as the gold standard.)
  3. Final check (checklist below), then submit all entries in the Week 10 Moodle thread by the deadline announced on Moodle.

Sample entry you can imitate

Week 8 — Entry 3 (sample, ~170 words)

Website setup: The reflection status page told me straight away that my link was error because my doc was private — that was useful, because before I had no idea whether anyone could read my work. What confused me: I did not know whether the journal thread or the status page counts as “submitted”, so I posted in both. One change: show the Week 8 / 9 / 10 deadline next to each entry row.

Partnership map. I did: chose which of our three info-request options to send, and cut the AI’s draft from 400 to 180 words. AI did: first draft, a table of what we had tried, spelling. Peers and teacher did: Wing flagged that our request went to the wrong department; the teacher’s example letter showed the tone.

Where I added value: the AI’s draft sounded like a complaint; my edit made it a request under the Access to Information regime — that judgment came from our class discussion, not from the model.

Wow / Aha. Wow: the AI produced a plausible citation that did not exist — I checked and it was invented. Aha: I can now use the same check-then-send workflow for my group’s second request.

Prompt you can give AI to draft your entry (then revise it yourself):

Here are my Moodle replies and notes for this week (paste them).
Draft a reflective journal entry of about 150 words with three short
headings: (1) website/setup feedback, (2) partnership map — I did /
AI did / peers and teacher did, (3) one Wow or Aha moment.
Write in the first person, plain English, no invented facts, no
citations. Leave anything you don't know marked as [ASK ME].

Then: answer every [ASK ME], delete anything that does not sound like you, add one concrete detail (a real task, a real name or tool), and check every claim against what actually happened.

Quick checklist (every entry)

  • ☐ About 150 words or more; written in the first person.
  • ☐ Drafted with AI if you like, but reviewed and revised by you — no sentence you cannot explain.
  • ☐ Contains a Wow and/or Aha moment, or (Week 10) a lasting change.
  • ☐ Written in the correct Google Doc tab, doc shared as “Anyone with the link can view”.
  • ☐ Link pasted at gcap3056.simonsays.hk/reflection/ and status is ok.
  • ☐ Final version posted in the Moodle forum thread for that week.
